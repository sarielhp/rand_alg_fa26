\begin {lemma} \lemlab {unique:inverse}Consider a prime $p$, and any numbers $x, y \in \ZZ _p$. If $x \neq y$ then, for each pair of numbers $r,s \in \ZZ _{p} = \{0,1, \ldots , p-1\}$, there is exactly one unique choice of numbers $a,b \in \ZZ _p$ such that $ax + b \equiv r \pmod p$ and $ay + b \equiv s \pmod p$. \end {lemma}
