\begin {lemma} \lemlab {n:t:silly:1}Let $p$ be a prime number. \begin {compactenumA} \item For any $\alpha , \beta \in \brc { 1, \ldots , p-1}$, we have that $\alpha \beta \not \equiv 0 \pmod {p}$. \par \item For any $\alpha , \beta , i \in \brc { 1,\ldots , p-1}$, such that $\alpha \ne \beta $, we have that $\alpha i \nequiv \beta i \pmod {p}$. \par \par \item For any $x \in \brc {1,\ldots , p-1}$ there exists a unique $y$ such that $xy \equiv 1 \pmod {p}$. The number $y$ is the \emphi {inverse} of $x$, and is denoted by $x^{-1}$ or $1/x$. \end {compactenumA} \end {lemma}
